# Why Step 2 failed, ranked by evidence

The original result remains authoritative for its experiment: 31/64 interventions found, 202 unmatched alerts, precision 13.3%, recall 48.4%, 84 alerts in 32 unchanged matches. Step 2.5 uses fresh families and a broader bidirectional target; its new baseline result is a separate comparison, not a revision of the old benchmark.

1. **Target observability is incomplete.** In 24 new development families, strong bundles move the measured line by +11.80m versus paired nulls, but press-only changes average +0.10m; carrier distance and density show no consistent strong-press effect. The engine setting called press affects several latent rules without necessarily changing observable carrier proximity. A classifier cannot recover information not reliably manifested in these inputs. Objective A (external policy) and B (observed shape) are different problems.

2. **Event-only spatial outcomes are noisy, downstream and context dependent.** Strong-bundle recovery-location separation is weaker than line geometry (development rank AUC 0.809 versus 1.000); moderate is 0.720 versus 0.887. Press-only event location is also weak (0.573). Recoveries mix simulator conventions, not uniformly successful presses. Natural score/possession/roster/late-game adaptation changes event windows without external intervention. On the new development primary set, the unchanged event baseline emits 277 unmatched alerts, 106 wrong-team alerts and 112 early alerts; these categories overlap. Null alerts are 59/24=2.458 per match. This is more than an algorithm-name issue.

3. **Halftime creates a structural latency floor.** The old engine clears period history and requires two full 480s windows plus two one-minute qualifying checks. `artifacts/old-halftime-audit.json` derives the earliest possible second-half alert from observed halftime in development matches. For 55-minute onsets, earliest eligibility is 3720–3840s, median minimum delay 480s; 60-minute onsets have median floor 180s, while 65/70-minute onsets have no necessary floor from warm-up alone. These are eligibility bounds, not actual detection delays. The new bounded reference can start at the observed period boundary once comparable-context support accrues; it does not retain oppositely oriented first-half coordinates or disable period handling.

4. **Correlated changes and repeated checks inflate apparent evidence.** Recovery mean and high-share are related. New deepest-three and q20 line features are nearly perfectly correlated; deepest-four correlation is 0.884 across supported development changes. They are unsuitable as independent confirming votes. Overlapping 30s checks are not independent hypothesis tests; persistence and hysteresis suppress repeated notifications but do not yield an analytical false-alarm probability. Full-match null evaluation remains necessary.

5. **Opportunity and timing matter.** Only 156/168 development five-minute tracking comparisons have sufficient common-context support; low-block paired analyses retain only 19/24 families. A change can reduce the very opponent build-up opportunities needed to measure it. Sparse cases remain in detector evaluation. The 60s ablation delays some decisions but does not solve false positives; lowering the cadence alone cannot recover an invisible press-only shift.

6. **Frequency/support definitions can masquerade as improvement.** All 28 development cadence pairs have identical integer-second frames/events. Only 11/28 have identical alert schedules if the same frame-count support minima are used at 2Hz. Doubling 2Hz minima to approximate equal exposure increases agreement to 25/28. More samples change support eligibility; they do not create independent evidence. The final pipeline stays frozen at 1Hz.

The selected fused rule asks for a large observed line movement plus event confirmation. It improves specificity at the cost of weak/moderate changes and can still alert on endogenous movement. It is a measured trade-off, not a solution to hidden coaching-intent inference. No old assertion, support guard or failure denominator was removed to improve the score.

## Goal/halftime proximity audit

On the new development baseline, 5/299 primary alerts are within120s of a goal and25/299 within300s of halftime; all25 are before halftime, zero after. Goal proximity is therefore not an evidenced dominant explanation for these alerts. The absent immediate post-half alerts are consistent with the warm-up gate, not evidence that the match is statistically quiet. These are overlapping descriptive categories (event-baseline-context.json), not causal attributions.

The selected unchanged false example also has only eight recent eligible frames, ending132s before its decision. Repeated checks can reuse sparse observations. Persistence is a notification guard, not a guarantee of fresh independent football episodes; no holdout repair was made.
